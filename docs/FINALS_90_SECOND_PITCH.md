# Second Look: 90-second finals pitch

Open [the finals slides](https://secondlook.ac/finals/). This is a separate pitch for a possible final round, not a replacement for the submitted three-minute script.

Audience: Deal Flow judges. Time: 90 seconds, requested by Mat. The comparison is between two offers for the same fund interest. It is not a ranking of different funds.

## Before presenting

Open the slides at slide 1. Use Right Arrow or Next twice. No sign-in, typing, provider calls or shared-case resets are needed. The screenshots are from the working app using an isolated fictional example, captured October 24. Say that out loud as written below.

Download [the three-slide PDF](https://secondlook.ac/finals/second-look-finals.pdf) as a network fallback. Rehearse aloud with a timer on the actual laptop. This script has 183 spoken words; if it runs long, omit the sentence about email threads. Timing is a target until the presenter rehearses it.

## 0:00–0:20: Would you take the higher offer?

**Show:** Slide 1. Point to the two purchase prices.

**Say:**

Imagine you’re selling your share of a fund. One buyer offers $700,000. Another offers $760,000. You’d probably take the higher offer. But what if that means giving up money the fund is about to pay you?

## 0:20–0:45: First, who keeps the fund payout?

**Show:** Slide 2. Point to the actual saved question beside the source. Do not read the entire question aloud.

**Say:**

We built Second Look to bring those missing details into the decision. These are screenshots from our working app, using a fictional fund. We start with outside research, here about a video game company, and turn it into a question for the fund. We keep the answer with the offer instead of losing it in an email thread.

## 0:45–1:30: The lower offer leaves you $40,000 more.

**Show:** Slide 3. Point to $800,000, $760,000 and the $40,000 difference. Finish here.

**Say:**

In this example, the fund confirms a $100,000 payout. The first offer lets you keep it, so you receive $800,000. The second gives it to the buyer, leaving you $760,000. That’s $40,000 less, before tax. Change the answer or terms, and the earlier checks need reviewing. Akta supplies company research, Nicrron drafts the question, Roundtable keeps the research history, and PRISM traces the AI call. We want to build this into the place investors and their advisers review every fund sale, from the first question to the final terms.

## If the judge asks

**Why not just use Claude?** “An AI can help read the documents. Here, the fund answer, the sale terms and the checks stay together. When an answer changes, the app knows the earlier checks need reviewing.”

**How were the sponsors used?** Expand the optional sponsor receipts below the slides. Akta supplied saved company context, Nicrron drafted the question, Roundtable saved versioned research tasks, and PRISM recorded the model call. This does not claim Ninoo hosting or Marketplace qualification.

**What would customers pay for?** “Our starting customer is an investor or adviser reviewing fund sales. We want to charge for keeping that review together across offers, people and changes. Pricing and willingness to pay still need validation.”
