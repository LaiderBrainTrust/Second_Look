# Second Look — founder brief

Track 4: Funds · Deal Flow, October 24, 2026 · Updated against merged PRs 13–15. This replaces older implementation-status and market-pitch wording in the shared planning document. Supporting claim checks: [SUBMISSION_CLAIMS.md](SUBMISSION_CLAIMS.md).

**Product.** Second Look helps an investor review an offer for a venture-fund interest, get the important questions answered, and recognize when changed terms invalidate the earlier review.

**Customer and problem.** Our initial customer hypothesis is a family-office investment professional or LP advisory team reviewing secondary-sale offers. They must reconcile old fund reports, outside evidence, manager answers and changing sale terms. The work spans people and time; a polished analysis can become outdated before the deal closes. Manager and buyer participants contribute answers and terms without receiving the investor's private research.

**Working example.** Start with a fictional gaming fund. Inspect dated Steam signals and Akta company research, then use a Roundtable task and Nicrron model call, traced through PRISM, to propose a manager question. The investor chooses what to share. A prepared fictional answer establishes a scenario assumption; deterministic code compares the offer. If the buyer changes who receives a $100,000 distribution, modeled gross receipts change from $800,000 to $700,000 and the previous review becomes historical. This is a difference between authored contract scenarios, not money earned or saved. Gaming signals do not prove fund ownership, distributions or fair value. The base example excludes fees, taxes and timing; the separate funding comparison uses explicitly entered dates and cash assumptions.

**Customer evidence.** Verified interview counts, permissioned quotes and committed pilots have not been supplied for this brief. We make no claim of validated demand, paying customers or revenue. The next discovery step is to observe a recent changed-term review with its actual operator and identify what their current tools miss.

**Market and why now.** Jefferies reports $125 billion of LP-led secondary transactions in 2025. That is transaction volume, not our software market size. It establishes a substantial underlying activity; we have not validated the number of reachable buying teams or their annual software budgets. APIs and model-assisted research let a small team build the evidence-gathering portion, while the product retains human responses, offer versions and review history. [Jefferies, published February 10, 2026](https://www.jefferies.com/insights/leadership-spotlight/2025-global-secondary-market-review-another-record-breaking-year/)

**Competition and differentiation hypothesis.** Existing alternatives include diligence platforms, fund administration and transfer tools, and an analyst using AI, email and spreadsheets. The hypothesis to test is whether our specific game evidence → manager answer → cash assumption → changed-offer review is faster and clearer for this buyer. Shared diligence, AI research and MCP are not exclusive capabilities. See [competitive review](COMPETITION.md).

**Business model and first customers.** Test a team subscription against per-case pricing. The earlier $500/team/month figure is an unvalidated hypothesis, not an agreed price or evidence of willingness to pay. Reach prospective family-office and secondary-advisory teams through event conversations and existing introductions; ask them to review a redacted completed case before proposing a scoped pilot. The first ten prospects and any commitments remain to be established.

**What is built.** A publicly hosted prototype with persistent cases, version checks, offer comparison, sponsor integrations and an authenticated synthetic presentation. Individual accounts, case permissions and invitations exist in the backend. Per-investor X1 OAuth consent, read-only source search, selected import and provider-confirmed disconnect passed on secondlook.ac using a dedicated account with fictional PDFs. The app suite passed 82 tests, follow-up connection checks and a production build. Ryan's individual sign-in/case-selection UI and the complete presenter rehearsal still need verification. This is not complete customer onboarding, transaction execution or a production-readiness claim. Existing X1 and sponsor services are dependencies; the new application code was built on October 24.
