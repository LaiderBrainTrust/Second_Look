# Second Look: 90-second live finals pitch

**Recommended format: show the app.** The screenshot slides are a fallback. The live story is about a deal changing after someone has reviewed it, and the app catching the financial consequence.

This condenses the original submitted story. It uses the same $700,000 offer and fictional $100,000 fund payout. It does not require adding a second offer or running fresh research during the pitch.

## Get the starting screen ready before the timer

Use an isolated rehearsal case or coordinate with the team before changing their shared example. Open Second Look and sign into the team example. The presenter operates the fictional investor and buyer roles; do not describe this as two independently authenticated people.

The case must have:

- A $700,000 purchase payment, zero price adjustment, and sale terms saying the investor keeps the fund payout.
- A saved fictional fund answer confirming $100,000, with its supporting example notice.
- No unresolved requirements preventing the review from being saved.
- A saved review for these exact terms.

Use the original three-minute script to prepare the answer if needed. Choose **Review & save**, resolve any listed missing details, and click **Save this review**. Start the pitch on that screen, with **$800,000** and **Your review is saved** visible. Locate **Try changing the buyer’s terms** before starting.

Do not claim this starting state has been prepared on the shared production case. It must be checked on the presenting laptop. Run this entire sequence aloud once with a 90-second timer; the timing below is a target, not a completed dress rehearsal.

## 0:00–0:25: Start with the investor’s money

**Show:** The saved review and $800,000 total. No typing.

**Say:**

> Imagine you’re selling your share of a fund. The buyer offers $700,000. You’ve asked the fund about an upcoming payout, and they’ve confirmed another $100,000. The sale terms let you keep it, so you expect $800,000 before taxes and fees. This is our fictional example in the working app, and we’ve already saved our review.

## 0:25–0:50: Change one real input

**Click:** **Try changing the buyer’s terms**. Under **Who keeps the fund payout under the sale agreement?**, select **The buyer of the fund interest**. In **Supporting source**, replace the text with **Fictional revised offer**. Leave the purchase payment and adjustment unchanged. Click **Save revised sale terms**, then **See the investor’s updated total**.

**Say while clicking:**

> Now I’m showing the buyer changing one term. The price stays exactly the same, but they want the fund payout. Watch what happens when we save that change.

## 0:50–1:10: Let the consequence land

**Show:** Point to **$100,000.00 less for you** and **Your previous review no longer applies**. Pause for two seconds. Stay on this screen for the rest of the pitch.

**Say:**

> We’re down to $700,000. Second Look catches the $100,000 difference and flags our old review. The earlier record stays there, but it no longer covers this deal. That’s the problem we’re solving: keeping people’s answers and decisions attached to the terms they actually checked.

## 1:10–1:30: Explain the build and the opportunity

**Show:** Keep the changed result visible. No navigation.

**Say:**

> Akta brings in company research, Nicrron helps draft the fund question, Roundtable keeps the research history, and PRISM traces the AI call. We want investors and their advisers to use Second Look every time a fund sale changes, so an expensive detail doesn’t get lost between the documents and the decision.
